## Minimum usability boundary

The [M1 milestone](multiphysics-roadmap.md#m1--minimum-usable-multiphysics-engine), established on 22 October 2026, defines the finite acceptance scope and owns its evidence. D1 establishes deduction readiness for three fixed nonspatial coupled cases; passing D1 ends general kernel polishing and moves implementation into Transform/Solve. U1 then verifies compilation of those exact derived graphs, optional scaling, numerical references, conservation, invalidation, Results and the existing browser workflow. Neither gate is certified merely by establishing the objective.

Primary responsibility spans Model → Transform → Solve → Results. Model supplies typed laws, premises, conditions, units and replay; Transform supplies the identity or selected supported formulation; Solve consumes its exact revision and publishes an admitted run; Results reconstructs and verifies that run. Physical/operation/unit changes invalidate dependent formulations and results. Existing execution limitations remain in force until the U1 bridge is implemented and tested. General symbolic completeness, unused adapter migration, general DAEs, spatial work and full-column capability are outside this minimum.

## Mandatory product workflow

### Operation validity after the independent audit

The shared kernel keeps generated denominator, isolation and calculus conditions as typed predicates, separately from explanatory reasons. Substitution specializes inherited guards, checks recorded domains and rejects proved contradictions; graph insertion rejects non-finite and non-real numeric expressions. Even-denominator rational powers retain nonnegative-radicand guards, including before parser cancellation; logarithms in kernel expressions retain positive-argument guards. The expression editor still accepts arithmetic and declared quantities only. Calculus requires an explicitly dependent quantity and records that the source equation holds throughout the chosen coordinate interval. This premise does not follow from an equality at one point. Real logarithmic primitives use absolute values and retain their excluded zeros.

Primary workspace: Model. Inputs are the selected equations, dependencies, units, domain premises and operation history; outputs are derived equations and their typed guards. The existing worksheet renders those guards through its assumptions rows. Transform receives the same rows; an operation introducing a new validity premise requires numerical enforcement before Solve can admit it. The source fingerprint is `physical-equation-graphs-8`. Changed mathematics or guards invalidate dependent operations, formulations and runs; labels do not. Invalid saved histories remain inspectable and reversible, without execution of a valid prefix.

This is scoped contradiction detection, not a general theorem prover or completed numerical guard evaluation. Existing physical reasons written as prose remain prose and are not parsed into invented predicates. M1 D1/U1 remain open; the roadmap records the audit findings, repair evidence and remaining acceptance work.

The internal M1 acceptance documents exercise coupling through these same controls: M1-A declares balanced component boundary flows and stream enthalpy/heat relations before caloric differentiation; M1-B substitutes wall resistance, inventory-derived pressure and path continuity before isolating the flow rate; M1-C connects two first laws through an explicit heat relation before caloric differentiation and mass/energy-rate substitution. The frozen inputs, operation maps, independent references and criterion-by-criterion D1 verdict live in the roadmap's fixed acceptance suite. These are internal documents, not product templates or another worksheet. Their admitted reduced relations and scalar conditions now use the versioned shared numerical bridge described below; unsupported lowering still blocks Solve, with no older thermal/hydraulic model as fallback. The used-control prefix audit distinguishes actual journal chronology from proofs rebuilt in dependency order; the first-law producer is unchanged and its pressure/kinetic commutation is enforced on actual expressions. The used storage path now derives pressure from all three local Cauchy components and their zero-rate conditions, including horizontal pressure independence. Its wall closure explicitly remains a constitutive premise without a claimed fundamental-law projection. Other adapters are not migrated. Claude independently approved all five D1 criteria on 23 October 2026; no U1 pass is claimed.

The user creates a **model**, never a sheet. Every simulation uses the same four connected workspaces:

1. **Model:** fundamental physical laws, explicit assumptions, components, topology, geometry, constitutive relations, thermodynamics, and the resulting mathematical problem. Preserve the mathematical and algebraic derivation. Do not put run buttons or numerical-method selection here.
2. **Transform:** optional mathematical techniques applied to that problem. Nondimensionalization is one technique, alongside spatial discretization, variable substitutions and future reductions or reformulations. Each technique declares its input requirements, validity, and whether it preserves exact equivalence or introduces approximation. No technique is mandatory merely because it appears first.
3. **Solve:** numerical data, remaining degrees of freedom, initial/boundary values, method selection, tolerances, initialization and execution. Physical meaning and assumptions remain defined in Model; numerical values are completed here on the same document. Matrix/Jacobian assembly depends on the selected method and current state when necessary.
4. **Results:** final solutions, charts, tables, exports, conservation residuals and numerical diagnostics. Distinguish numerical convergence from discretization accuracy and physical validation.

Before implementing any new request, identify its primary workspace, shared services, producers, consumers and invalidation rules. Reuse the common model, units, compiler, solvers, thermodynamic layer and results format. A new simulation is data and assumptions flowing through this framework, not a new application or a solver hidden in its example page.

### Connected formulation and execution

Transform extends the established model with derived mathematical formulations. It preserves the physical definition and its provenance within the same model document. The intended execution path is **Model definition → selected Transform chain (or identity) → compilation in Solve → Results with reconstruction to physical variables**.

- Every transformation consumes a specific formulation revision and produces a derived formulation with its operation, settings, validity, and variable/domain/condition maps. Approximate operations also identify the approximation and its verification requirements.
- Operations can be composed when their input requirements are satisfied; nondimensionalization and discretization have no universally fixed order. With no selected transformation, the identity path passes the physical formulation to Solve.
- Solve compiles the selected derived formulation, including its applicable closures and constraints. It must identify the formulation being executed and reject unsupported execution rather than silently run a different formulation.
- Results identifies the executed formulation and transformation chain, reconstructs physical variables where supported, and retains diagnostics for the original physical problem as well as the numerical formulation.
- Changes invalidate dependent artifacts through their signatures: physical definition → derived formulations → compiled problem → results. A transformation change does not rewrite the physical definition; numerical settings invalidate the execution artifacts that depend on them.

This is the required integration direction. The first scaling execution bridge is implemented for supported time-only models, as documented below; the existing mesh setting feeds the supported spatial solver. Arbitrary transformation chains remain a future capability.

## Model derivation standard and audit

### Composable unit operations (user decision, 7 November 2026, `dec-composable-operations`)

Every unit operation is deduced once, generically, and assembled; combinations are never deduced as new models. The rule applies to all operations (reactors, separators, heat transfer, pressure change, mixing and splitting), to Claude, Codex and the Gemini research dossiers.

1. **One deduction per operation class.** A class starts from the fundamental component-mass, energy and momentum laws over its control volume. Its variants are recorded assumptions on that one deduction, not separate models. Example: batch (closed boundary), semi-batch (feed only), CSTR (open boundary, perfect mixing) and PFR (axial spatial resolution; deduction only while spatial execution is deferred) are variants of one reacting control volume.
2. **The operation's own energy balance is intrinsic.** It is always deduced in the same control volume, because thermodynamics is always present. Isothermal operation is an assumption recorded on that balance.
3. **Services are separate templates.** A jacket, coil, external heat exchanger, electric heater, pump, valve or compressor has its own control volume and its own deduction, and exposes typed ports (`material`, `heat`, `work`). It never becomes part of another operation's deduction.
4. **Coupling happens only at a shared boundary.** One constitutive relation is declared at the boundary (for example Q = U A (T_service - T_process)). Its flow enters both balances with opposite signs, so the coupling conserves energy or mass by construction. A prescribed service state (for example a known jacket temperature) is the limiting case of that boundary relation, recorded as an assumption.
5. **Assembly is topology.** Templates are connected port to port in the flowsheet, never wired inside Model.

Current state (7 November 2026): the CSTR of Demo 0.2 uses the limiting case (constitutive U A (T_j - T) with a prescribed jacket temperature, injected through the generic `heat_builder` hook and exposed as a `heat` port). Missing, recorded as Engine findings: a service template with its own control volume (first, a dynamic jacket), and heat or work connections between templates: `core/modelo/flowsheet.py` connects only `material` ports (`flowsheet_port_kind`).
